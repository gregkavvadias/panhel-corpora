# Equally Frequent, More Abstract: Reading Texts in the Greek Lyceum English Coursebooks and the Panhellenic English Examination

## Abstract

This study compares the reading texts of the two Greek state Lyceum English coursebooks (27 texts) with the reading passages of the Panhellenic English examination (26 passages, 2010–2026). I used open, reproducible measures of vocabulary, syntax and cohesion, designated four primary measures, and tested lexical coverage for equivalence. By word frequency the two sets are equivalent. The examination texts, however, are markedly more abstract (concreteness *g* = −1.15). Descriptively, they also use fewer passives and more clauses per sentence. Readability formulas detected none of these differences. Every examination passage is informational or argumentative, while 12 of the coursebook texts are narratives. Yet genre does not explain the difference: the coursebooks' own non-narrative texts remain more concrete and use more passives. The coursebooks thus offer texts that match the examination's in vocabulary frequency but differ from it in kind.

**Keywords:** text difficulty, lexical coverage, concreteness, genre, coursebooks, high-stakes testing, Greece
