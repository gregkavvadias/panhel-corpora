## Abstract

This study compares the reading texts of the two Greek state Lyceum English coursebooks (27 texts) with the reading passages of the Panhellenic English examination (26 passages, 2010–2026). I used open, reproducible measures of vocabulary, syntax and cohesion, fixed four primary measures in advance, and tested lexical coverage for equivalence. By word frequency the two sets are equivalent. The examination texts, however, are markedly more abstract (concreteness *g* = −1.15). They also use fewer passives and contain more, shorter clauses with more explicit linking between sentences. Readability formulas detected none of these differences. Every examination passage is expository or argumentative, while 12 of the coursebook texts are narratives. Yet genre does not explain the difference: the coursebooks' own non-narrative texts remain more concrete and more passive. The coursebooks thus provide reading material of the right frequency but the wrong kind.

**Keywords:** text difficulty, lexical coverage, concreteness, genre, coursebooks, high-stakes testing, Greece
